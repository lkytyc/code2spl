`EmailClient` models a simple mailbox with a fixed storage capacity. Each instance has an address, a capacity limit, and an inbox list.

**Sending mail (`send_to`):** The sender asks the recipient to check whether adding the new email would exceed its capacity. If there's room, it constructs an email dict — recording sender, receiver, content, size, a current timestamp, and an "unread" state — and appends it to the recipient's inbox, returning `True`. If the recipient is full, the sender clears its *own* inbox to free at least `size` worth of space and returns `False`. Note: the email is not retried after clearing; the send simply fails.

**Fetching mail (`fetch`):** Scans the inbox from the beginning and returns the first email whose state is "unread", flipping it to "read" before returning. If the inbox is empty or all messages are already read, it returns `None`.

**Capacity checking (`is_full_with_one_more_email`, `get_occupied_size`):** `get_occupied_size` sums the `size` field of every email in the inbox. `is_full_with_one_more_email` uses that total to check whether adding `size` more bytes would exceed capacity, returning a boolean.

**Clearing the inbox (`clear_inbox`):** Removes emails from the front of the inbox one by one until the freed space meets or exceeds the requested `size`. There's a guard that exits early if `self.addr` is empty, which appears to be a bug — it likely intended to check `self.inbox` instead.

The main quirk is that a failed send triggers a cleanup on the *sender's* inbox, not the recipient's, which is an unusual design choice and probably not intentional.
