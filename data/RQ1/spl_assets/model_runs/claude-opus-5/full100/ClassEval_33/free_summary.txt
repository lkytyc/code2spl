`DiscountStrategy` models a purchase order and its pricing logic in a single class.

**Construction and data:** The class takes a `customer` dict, a `cart` list of dicts (each with `product`, `quantity`, and `price` keys), and an optional `promotion` callable. On init it immediately computes and caches the cart total.

**`total()`** sums `quantity * price` across all cart items and stores the result in the private `__total` attribute, returning it each time it's called.

**`due()`** determines the final amount owed. If no promotion was supplied, no discount is applied. Otherwise it calls the promotion function, passing the order itself as the argument, and subtracts the returned discount from `__total`.

**The three promotion strategies** are static methods, each accepting an order object and returning a discount amount:

- `FidelityPromo` — gives 5% off the whole order if the customer's `fidelity` score is at least 1000, otherwise nothing.
- `BulkItemPromo` — gives 10% off any individual line item where the quantity is 20 or more, summing discounts across all qualifying items.
- `LargeOrderPromo` — gives 7% off the whole order if the cart contains at least 10 distinct products (by `product` key), otherwise nothing.

**How they connect:** A promotion is passed in as a plain callable at construction time, so the three static methods can be used interchangeably as the `promotion` argument. Each one receives the `DiscountStrategy` instance and calls `total()` or reads `cart` directly from it to compute its discount. This is the Strategy pattern — the algorithm varies while the `due()` call site stays the same.
