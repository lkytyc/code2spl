`StockPortfolioTracker` manages a simple stock portfolio with a cash balance. Here's what it does:

**State**
- `portfolio`: a list of stock dicts, each with at least `name`, `quantity`, and `price` fields.
- `cash_balance`: a float representing available cash, set at construction.

**Core methods**

`add_stock(stock)` — merges a stock into the portfolio by name. If the name already exists, it increments the quantity; otherwise appends a new entry.

`remove_stock(stock)` — decrements a stock's quantity by the requested amount, provided the holding exists and has sufficient quantity. Removes the entry entirely if quantity hits zero. Returns `True` on success, `False` if the stock isn't found or the quantity is insufficient.

`buy_stock(stock)` — checks whether `price × quantity` exceeds the cash balance. If it would, returns `False` without changing state. Otherwise, calls `add_stock` and deducts the total cost from the cash balance.

`sell_stock(stock)` — delegates to `remove_stock`. If that fails, returns `False`. On success, adds `price × quantity` back to the cash balance.

`calculate_portfolio_value()` — sums `price × quantity` across all holdings and adds the cash balance, returning the total portfolio value.

`get_portfolio_summary()` — builds a list of `{name, value}` dicts for each holding (using `get_stock_value`), then calls `calculate_portfolio_value` and returns both the total value and the summary list as a tuple.

`get_stock_value(stock)` — returns `price × quantity` for a single stock dict.

**Notable behavior**
- Stock prices are not stored or tracked internally; they come in on every operation via the stock dict. This means the price used for buying can differ from the price used when selling the same holding.
- There's no validation that quantity or price are positive, and no deduplication on case or whitespace in stock names.
- `get_portfolio_summary` calls `get_stock_value` using the stock's stored price, which may be stale if the price changed between buy and the summary call.
